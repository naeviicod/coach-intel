# Coach Intel --- Brand Identity

## 1. Brand Positioning

**Coach Intel** is a competitive intelligence and coaching platform for
esports organizations, coaches, analysts, and teams.

It turns fragmented player, team, match, map, and performance data into
an organized intelligence system that helps teams understand
performance, identify trends, scout opponents, compare players, and make
better competitive decisions.

**Positioning:** Esports × Sports Science × Tactical Intelligence ×
Premium SaaS

**Core idea:** The intelligence layer behind competitive esports teams.

The brand should feel closer to F1 telemetry, professional sports
scouting, an analyst command center, and a premium data platform than a
conventional gaming stats app.

Avoid childish gaming graphics, excessive neon, military cosplay,
generic AI gradients, and visual imitation of individual game
franchises.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Brand Name

# COACH INTEL

The name is intentionally broader than a single game. Call of Duty can
be the initial/core title while the platform architecture and brand
remain ready for additional competitive games.

**Category:** Competitive Intelligence Platform\
**Primary descriptor:** Competitive Intelligence for Esports\
**Call of Duty descriptor:** Competitive Intelligence for Call of Duty\
**Primary tagline:** **Know More. Win More.**\
**Supporting line:** **Every player. Every map. Every advantage.**\
**Product philosophy:** **Data → Context → Insight → Action**\
**Core promise:** **Turn performance data into competitive advantage.**

Give competitive gaming organizations the intelligence they need to make
better decisions about players, teams, strategy, scouting, and

Competitive teams generate enormous amounts of information, but much of
it remains scattered across spreadsheets, websites, screenshots,
conversations, and individual coaches.

Coach Intel turns that information into one organized competitive
intelligence system.

## 6. Brand Promise

**Turn performance data into competitive advantage.**

## 8. Game Selector

Coach Intel should be designed as a multi-game platform even if Call of
Duty is the first and primary implementation.

### Placement

The active game should be visible in the main application header,
preferably near the Coach Intel identity or organization/team selector.

Example:

`COACH INTEL  /  CALL OF DUTY ▾`

or:

`Game: Call of Duty ▾`

### Game Selector Behavior

The selector should:

-   Set the active competitive game context globally.
-   Filter organizations, teams, players, matches, maps, modes,
    statistics, reports, and Intel to the selected game.
-   Preserve game-specific terminology and metrics.
-   Allow a player or organization to participate in multiple supported
    games without mixing incompatible statistics.
-   Remember the user's last selected game.
-   Clearly show when a module or metric is game-specific.
-   Keep the overall Coach Intel navigation and design system consistent
    between games.
-   Support adding future titles without redesigning the core platform.

### Initial Game

**Call of Duty**

Potential future expansion can support other competitive titles while
keeping each game's data model and terminology isolated where necessary.

### Game-Aware Experience

Changing the game should update relevant areas such as:

-   Teams and rosters
-   Players
-   Matches
-   Maps
-   Modes
-   Performance metrics
-   Rankings
-   Scouting
-   Intel
-   Reports
-   Comparison tools
-   Game-specific dashboards

The game selector should not merely be a cosmetic filter. It should
define the active competitive data environment.

Avoid predictable esports imagery such as controllers, weapons, skulls,
shields, or literal crosshairs.

The primary logo concept should be based on **CI --- Coach Intel**.

### Concept

Create a geometric **C** from segmented data/telemetry forms with a
strong central **I** or intelligence marker.

The mark should communicate:

-   Intelligence
-   Data
-   Analysis
-   Precision
-   Competition
-   Forward movement

It must remain recognizable as:

-   Full wordmark
-   App icon
-   Favicon
-   Dashboard mark
-   Social avatar
-   Report branding
-   Monochrome mark

The identity should look appropriate on an elite esports analyst's
workstation rather than only on a gaming jersey.

## 15. Brand Voice

Coach Intel should communicate like an elite performance analyst: short,
precise, confident, and data-driven.

Avoid conversational AI language.

Instead of:

> Great job! Your team has been performing much better recently!

Use:

> **Hardpoint performance is trending upward. Win rate increased 11.4%
> across the last 10 maps.**

Instead of:

> We noticed you may be struggling on Control.

Use:

> **Control defense identified as the primary performance gap.**

The product should not sound like a chatbot. It should sound like an
elite analyst.

# Product Plan Additions

## 18. Coach & Team Input Workspace

Coach Intel should include a collaborative input area where coaches,
analysts, and authorized team members can add qualitative information
that statistics alone cannot capture.

The goal is to combine **structured performance data + human coaching
intelligence** in one environment.

### Shared Team Notes

Each organization and team should have access to shared text-based
working documents.

Example use cases:

-   Scrim notes
-   Match notes
-   Player observations
-   Coaching feedback
-   Tactical notes
-   Map and mode notes
-   Opponent scouting
-   Practice objectives
-   Review notes
-   Post-match conclusions
-   Improvement priorities
-   General team intelligence

The experience should feel like a lightweight collaborative text
document inside Coach Intel rather than a complex document-management
system.

### Collaboration

Authorized coaches and team members should be able to work together on
the same text file/document.

The design should prepare for:

-   Shared editing
-   Autosave
-   Author attribution
-   Last edited information
-   Timestamps
-   Team-specific documents
-   Organization-level documents
-   Player-linked notes
-   Match-linked notes
-   Map/mode-linked notes
-   Simple document history/version awareness

Real-time collaboration can be treated as a future capability if it
unnecessarily increases the complexity of the first version.

### Voice / Microphone Input

Add a **microphone action** to the text input/editor so coaches can
quickly capture observations vocally.

The microphone should visually match the clean, minimal icon style used
throughout Coach Intel.

Suggested flow:

`Mic → Speak → Speech-to-text → Review transcript → Add/Edit → Save`

Voice input should never silently become permanent coaching data. The
coach should be able to review and edit the transcription before saving
it.

Useful scenarios include:

-   Recording thoughts directly after a scrim
-   Fast player observations
-   Post-match reviews
-   Tactical ideas
-   Scouting notes while watching VOD
-   Hands-free note capture

Future intelligence features may organize these notes, extract recurring
themes, connect observations to players/maps/matches, and surface
relevant coaching context in the Intel Feed.

## 19. Organization & Team Setup

Coach Intel needs a lightweight **Sign Up / Workspace Setup**
experience.

This does **not** need to behave like a complex organization database
during the initial product phase.

The purpose is primarily to establish the user's Coach Intel workspace
and organizational structure.

### Initial Setup

Suggested onboarding:

1.  Create Coach Intel account
2.  Enter organization name
3.  Add organization abbreviation/tag
4.  Add organization logo --- optional
5.  Select active game
6.  Create first team
7.  Enter team name
8.  Add team abbreviation/tag --- optional
9.  Add roster/player names
10. Select basic role/access level
11. Enter Command Center

### Example

**Organization**

`OpTic Gaming`

**Team**

`OpTic Texas`

**Game**

`Call of Duty`

The architecture should allow one organization to contain multiple teams
and, eventually, teams from multiple supported games.

### Organization Structure

``` text
ORGANIZATION
│
├── Team A
│   ├── Players
│   ├── Coaches
│   ├── Matches
│   ├── Performance
│   ├── Notes
│   └── Intel
│
├── Team B
│   └── ...
│
└── Organization Overview
```

Keep setup fast. Coach Intel should not require unnecessary corporate
information before the user can begin working.

## 20. Organization Overview

Each organization should have a dedicated high-level overview page.

This is the executive/competitive overview of everything happening
inside the organization.

### Organization Overview Should Include

-   Organization identity
-   Active game
-   Teams
-   Overall organization win rate
-   Total series record
-   Recent organization performance
-   Team comparison
-   Recent matches
-   Upcoming matches
-   Organization-wide performance trends
-   Top-performing players
-   Players trending upward/downward
-   Recent coaching notes
-   Important Intel signals
-   Team rankings
-   Map/mode performance where applicable
-   Quick navigation into each team

### Organization Win Rate

Organization-level win rate should aggregate the relevant results of its
teams while still allowing users to drill down.

Example:

``` text
ORGANIZATION PERFORMANCE

Overall Win Rate       68.4%
Series                 26–12
Maps                    94–61
Recent Form             W W L W W

TEAM PERFORMANCE

Main Roster             72.1%
Academy                  61.5%
Team 3                   58.8%
```

Filters should make clear which game, season, competition, date range,
and match type the calculation represents.

## 22. Win Rates & Competitive Records

Win rate needs to be a first-class Coach Intel metric.

It should be available at several levels.

### Organization Level

-   Overall organization win rate
-   Win rate by team
-   Win rate by game
-   Win rate by period/season
-   Recent-form win rate

### Team Level

-   Overall team win rate
-   Series win rate
-   Match win rate
-   Map win rate
-   Mode win rate
-   Map + mode combination
-   Opponent-specific win rate
-   Tournament/competition win rate
-   Recent-form win rate
-   Side/start-condition win rate where the game supports it

### Player Context

Player pages can show team performance while the player was active, but
Coach Intel should avoid presenting correlation as individual causation.

### Required Context

Every displayed win rate should make its scope understandable.

Example:

`HP WIN RATE  76.4%  · LAST 30 DAYS`

or:

`SERIES WIN RATE  68.2%  · 2026 SEASON`

This prevents misleading statistics.

## 23. Review System --- Needs Review

Coach Intel should include a clear **Needs Review** workflow.

This is especially useful for coaching observations, imported
information, voice transcripts, automatically generated Intel,
incomplete match information, and anything requiring human confirmation.

### Needs Review Can Apply To

-   Voice transcriptions
-   Coaching notes
-   Match data
-   Player information
-   Imported statistics
-   Intel observations
-   Scouting information
-   Automatically detected anomalies
-   Unverified information
-   Incomplete records

### Review States

Use simple states:

`NEEDS REVIEW`

`REVIEWED`

`ACTION REQUIRED`

`RESOLVED`

### Review Center

A centralized review queue should let coaches see everything requiring
attention.

Example:

``` text
NEEDS REVIEW                                      7

Voice Note       Main Roster / Scrim          4m ago
Match Data       Team A vs Team B             18m ago
Intel Signal     Control defense decline       1h ago
Player Note      Player A                      2h ago
Scouting Note    Upcoming opponent             3h ago
```

Items should link directly to their relevant team, player, match,
document, or Intel context.

## 26. Product Principle --- Human + Data Intelligence

Coach Intel should not attempt to replace the coach.

Its strongest position is combining what the system knows with what the
coaching staff knows.

### System Intelligence

-   Statistics
-   Trends
-   Win rates
-   Comparisons
-   Match history
-   Performance changes
-   Pattern detection
-   Scouting data

### Human Intelligence

-   Coaching observations
-   Tactical context
-   Player feedback
-   Scrim observations
-   Team dynamics
-   VOD notes
-   Voice notes
-   Match context

### Coach Intel

**DATA + COACH INPUT → CONTEXT → INTEL → REVIEW → ACTION**

This should become an important product philosophy alongside:

**Data → Context → Insight → Action**
